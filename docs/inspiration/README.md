# External Inspiration and Transfer Limits

## Purpose and outcomes

This chapter records practices worth studying without treating any organization as a national template. Transfer each practice only after stating its purpose, prerequisites, rights impact, context, and failure mode.

| Source | Practice to study | Possible PolisOS use | Transfer limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| [NASA Systems Engineering Handbook](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/systems-engineering-handbook/) | Requirements traceability, interfaces, verification, risk and lifecycle thinking | Trace each problem, project requirement, measure, owner, and acceptance evidence | Public systems involve contested values and rights; engineering cannot choose society’s ends |
| [OECD Guidelines for Citizen Participation](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-guidelines-for-citizen-participation-processes_f765caf6-en.html) | Purposeful participation, inclusive process design, evaluation | Choose participation method to fit the decision and publish how contributions affect it | Participation counts do not prove representativeness or legitimacy |
| [Toyota Production System](https://global.toyota/en/company/vision-and-philosophy/production-system/) | Continuous improvement, visible problems, quality at source, flow | Short feedback loops, milestone review, root-cause analysis, stopping to correct defects | A firm’s labor hierarchy, incentives, and profit goal cannot be copied as national governance |
| [Estonia X-tee](https://www.ria.ee/en/state-information-system/data-exchange-platforms/data-exchange-layer-x-tee) | Secure, logged, federated exchange between data owners | Service-to-service exchange with purpose, authentication, and audit | Interoperability does not create legal authority to combine person records; context and law differ |

## Design translation

Use a systems-engineering style for traceability and assurance, an OECD-style participation plan for public decisions, continuous improvement for service delivery, and federated exchange patterns where institutions retain control of their data. Keep human rights, privacy, lawful authority, and appeal as independent constraints.

## Control-loop interpretation

A feedback loop can organize observation, measurement, analysis, intervention, and evaluation. The analogy has limits: social outcomes have multiple causes, objectives conflict, feedback is delayed, and measurement can change behavior. Never optimize one metric without safeguards against gaming, distributional harms, and Goodhart effects.

## Review method

A proposed transfer records: original practice and context; demonstrated result and evidence; assumptions; affected groups; likely harms; legal fit; adaptations; success and stop criteria; independent evaluation plan. Case studies are not endorsements and should be reviewed periodically.
