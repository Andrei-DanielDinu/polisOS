# D20 Diplomacy and Foreign Relations

## 1. Purpose and outcomes

Track public treaty commitments, international cooperation, cross-border projects, trade context, and approved data exchange without claiming diplomatic authority.

## 2. Citizen experience

Citizens can read source-linked treaty and cooperation summaries, see public commitments and project outcomes, and contribute to consultations when a competent body opens them.

## 3. Roles, authority, and boundaries

Foreign affairs institutions and authorized officials speak for the state; trade and treaty obligations are verified against official sources; researchers can submit annotated analysis.

## 4. Workflow

Register official instrument → identify authority, parties, obligations and dates → link responsible institution → track public milestone → publish approved report and review.

Domain feedback cycle: observe → qualify source and uncertainty → measure or record evidence → review with affected people → propose options → authorize through the responsible institution → deliver → evaluate effects and correct. A measurement can request review; it cannot itself exercise legal authority.

## 5. Observation register

| What is observed | Source and steward | Cadence and uncertainty | Allowed use and prohibited trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Official treaty texts, government releases, international organization datasets, customs/trade aggregates and public project records. Update on official publication and periodic status reports; stewards state translation and cross-country comparability limits. | Named domain data steward and original source owner; affected people can submit corrections | Event-triggered for direct workflow and security records; source-native timing for institutional statistics; monthly freshness check; quarterly outcome review; annual independent method review. ## 6. Data and privacy

Use the minimum data needed for this domain. Citizen-level submissions are opt-in, purpose-labeled, and separate from authentication records. Prefer public, institutional, operational, and aggregate sources. No default cross-link to personal government records. Classify each record, set access and retention, allow correction, and suppress unsafe small aggregates. Data from this domain must not be reused for employment, credit, benefits, policing, or public ranking unless a separately lawful and reviewed purpose is established.

## 7. Measures and feedback

Commitment reporting completeness; project milestones; trade flows by aggregate category; data exchange availability. No automated assessment of geopolitical intent or diplomatic action.

Every indicator states definition, numerator/denominator where applicable, source, owner, geographic and population scope, update cadence, uncertainty, missingness, known confounders, revision policy, and the actions it may or may not support. Outcomes are reviewed with affected people and, for consequential decisions, an independent evaluator.

## 8. Failures, correction, and appeal

Outdated translation, misleading comparison, unauthorized disclosure, or platform content mistaken for state position. Label official sources, provide correction and contact route, distinguish analysis from government statement.

Provide a visible correction route, preserve provenance and revision history, and offer human review independent of the original decision when practicable. Publish a plain-language reason when a report is not accepted, a data item is corrected, or a workflow is stopped.

## 9. Integrations and contracts

Supply chains, economy, governance, public finance, legal texts, data interoperability and authorized institutions.

Use shared API and event rules from [service architecture](../../architecture/README.md). Exchange only the minimum necessary fields. Events must carry schema version and purpose; personal content is stored behind authorization and referenced by an opaque identifier.

## 10. Delivery phases

Foundation: source-linked public commitments. First workflow: local project with cross-border relevance. Later: approved interoperability and aggregate exchange.

## 11. Stack decision record

**Status: open for domain-owner selection.** The domain must remain independently deployable and conform to shared contracts.

No diplomacy or treaty platform selected. License: FOSS software and international dataset terms audited separately. Hosting/operations: translation, archival and secure government exchange cost money. Security: access controls and release review for non-public material. Interoperability: standard identifiers and documented open exchange. Migration: preserve official instruments and versioned references.

All selected components, versions, extensions, model weights, and datasets require a documented license and security review before adoption. Record hosting region, recurring operational burden, staffing, backup/recovery, export and replacement plan in the implementation ADR.

## 12. Open decisions

Authoritative treaty source; translation status; public versus restricted commitments; international data exchange approvals.
